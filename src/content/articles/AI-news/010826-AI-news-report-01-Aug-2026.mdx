## Executive Summary

OpenAI dominated the period with seven posts spanning infrastructure economics, benchmark methodology, scientific workflows, workplace adoption, and academic distribution, while Anthropic published one important protocol update focused on MCP deployability and governance. **Key trends:**

- AI vendors are increasingly selling outcome economics, workflow throughput, and infrastructure efficiency rather than raw model novelty alone.
- Agent performance is being framed as a systems problem where harness design, context handling, and tooling choices materially affect results.
- Enterprise adoption is maturing around governance and standardization, with more emphasis on privacy-controlled workspaces, interoperable protocols, and production-ready authorization.

### [How enabling two settings tripled our scores on the ARC-AGI-3 benchmark](https://openai.com/index/how-two-settings-tripled-our-arc-agi-3-scores/)
**Published:** 2026-07-29

OpenAI argues that GPT-5.6 Sol's weak ARC-AGI-3 result was mostly a harness problem rather than a pure model limitation. Preserving reasoning state and using compaction instead of rolling truncation raised the public-set score from 13.3 percent to 38.3 percent while using far fewer output tokens. The article is a strong reminder that agent benchmarks are deeply sensitive to API and harness design choices.

**Published:** 2026-07-28

Anthropic's MCP post is primarily an engineering infrastructure update. That makes MCP servers easier to run on conventional HTTP, serverless, and edge environments while making long-running workflows and inline UIs more standardized.
