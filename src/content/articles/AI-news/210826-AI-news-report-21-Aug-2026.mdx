## Executive Summary

This week was defined by two themes: tighter security controls around more capable AI systems, and broader organizational rollout into education, infrastructure, and production workflows. OpenAI emphasized cyber-risk pacing, private safety processing, teen protections, and large-scale infrastructure and policy initiatives, while Anthropic focused on agent-building primitives, cybersecurity deployment, and AI fluency as an operational capability. Across both companies, AI is being packaged less as a standalone model announcement and more as managed infrastructure with explicit governance boundaries.

**Key updates:**

- OpenAI said it slowed some frontier model development work after cyber-risk signals increased, adding tighter research, monitoring, and deployment controls.
- OpenAI introduced a preview path for Zero Data Retention customers to keep stronger frontier-model safety enforcement without exposing customer content to human review.
- Anthropic announced general availability for computer use, the Skills API, and the Files API, positioning them as a production stack for agentic workflows.
- Anthropic expanded defensive access to Claude Mythos 5 through Claude Security, partner integrations, and a new Defender Advantage Fund.
- OpenAI and Anthropic both pushed harder into education, with OpenAI launching ChatGPT for Teens and a CodeAI partnership while Anthropic formalized its internal AI-fluency approach through Claude Academy.
- OpenAI expanded its long-term footprint with the PORTS-Pike infrastructure project and associated Ohio student Codex credits.

**Key trends:**

- Security and safety controls are increasingly shipping as product features, not just internal policy commitments.
- AI vendors are treating education and workforce enablement as strategic adoption channels rather than side programs.
- Production AI announcements now combine models with infrastructure, workflow tooling, monitoring, and governance mechanisms.
